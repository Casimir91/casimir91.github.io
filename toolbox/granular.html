Waveshaper & Granular

Two ways to sculpt a sound: bend the waveform with a curve, or cut it into thousands of tiny grains.

1 · Waveshaper y = f(x)

Every sample x of the input goes through the curve and comes out as y = f(x). A straight line changes nothing; a bent line adds harmonics (look at the spectrum). Draw the curve with finger or mouse, pick a preset, or build it from Chebyshev polynomials: with a full-scale sine in, Tn(cos θ) = cos nθ, so each coefficient is exactly the amplitude of one harmonic.

Harmonic recipe · Chebyshev polynomials T1…T8

Set the amplitude of each harmonic: the curve becomes f(x) = Σ aₙ·Tₙ(x), with T₀=1, T₁=x, T₂=2x²−1, Tₙ₊₁=2x·Tₙ−Tₙ₋₁. Keep the input level at 100 % and a sine input for the exact result.

2 · Granular synthesis g(t) = w(t)·s(t+τ)

The sound is cut into grains a few milliseconds long, each shaped by a window w(t) and played back from a chosen position in the source. Thousands of grains per second blend into a continuous cloud. Move the position to scan through the source, scatter it with spray, change pitch without changing speed. Short grains blur the pitch: the uncertainty relation Δf ≈ 1/Δt.
