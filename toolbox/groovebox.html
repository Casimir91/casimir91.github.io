Drums
Brush

Ghost = a soft hit (funk snare). Roll = the step is split in two or three quick hits (trap hi-hat). Tap a filled step with the same brush to clear it.

Bass line

Each row is a note of the scale. Tap to place a note of the chosen length, or drag to the right to hold it longer: the note keeps sounding (sustain) until its end. A new note cuts the previous one.

Selected: Kick
Chord progression

The loop is 4 bars. Each bar shifts Bass, Chord stab, Pluck and the Chank string by some semitones: same pattern, another chord. After bar 4 it returns to bar 1. (Transposing by n semitones multiplies every frequency by 2n/12.)

Guitar, funk & arp

Chord stab and Pluck follow the bass: they play the note under the step (the key note when the bass is silent). Use the brush of the Drums section for ghost notes and rolls.

Arp plays the chord of the bass note one note at a time: each active step takes the next note. Order, number of notes and octaves are the chips above; the pitches follow the chord progression.

Keys · live synth
Octave C4

Dots mark the notes of the current key and scale. On a computer: A S D F G H J K L are the white keys, W E T Y U O P the black ones, Z/X change octave. Slide a finger across the keys for a glissando.

Loop station: Rec starts the loop if it is stopped and records every note exactly when you play it, over the 4 bars of the progression. Press Rec again to stop recording: the notes keep looping with the beat and follow the tempo. Rec again to overdub more.

Mixer & filters

Volume sets the loudness of each channel; Reverb send sends part of it into a virtual room.

Filters act on the whole mix. High-pass removes the lows (raise it for a thin, telephone sound), low-pass removes the highs (lower it for a muffled, underwater sound); resonance boosts the band around the cutoff. Reverb = the sound convolved with the “impulse response” of a room: noise that fades to −60 dB in the decay time. The oscilloscope shows the result.
