Three formulas

Kinetic energy
Ek = ½ m v²
Ek ∝ v²
∝ means directly proportional: double the speed, 4 × the energy.
Gravitational potential energy
ΔEp = m g Δh
ΔEp ∝ Δh
Twice as high, twice the energy.
Work
W = F × d
W ∝ F and W ∝ d
Force along the motion, times the distance moved.

All three are measured in joules (J), but they are not the same thing: kinetic and potential energy are stores, work is a transfer.

When are they equal?

BrakingEk = W = F × dAll the motion store becomes work done by the brakes.
Thrown up, or fallingEk = ΔEpMotion store and height store swap, with no air resistance.
Lifted slowly, or pushed by gravityΔEp = WThe work done is the height store gained or lost: W = m g Δh.

For those who want the numbers

Worked example, practice, and an exam-style sheet. Use \(g = 10\ \text{N/kg}\).

Worked example

A ball of \(0.20\ \text{kg}\) is dropped from \(1.8\ \text{m}\). Speed at the ground? No air.

Data. \(m = 0.20\ \text{kg}\), \(\Delta h = 1.8\ \text{m}\).

Energy. \(\Delta E_p = m g \Delta h = 0.20 \times 10 \times 1.8 = 3.6\ \text{J}\). All of it becomes \(E_k\).

Speed. \(\tfrac12 \times 0.20 \times v^2 = 3.6\), so \(v^2 = 36\) and \(v = 6.0\ \text{m/s}\) (about \(22\ \text{km/h}\)).

Check. Units are J and m/s. \(6\ \text{m/s}\) is a fast run: plausible. A heavier ball? Same speed: \(m\) cancels in \(m g \Delta h = \tfrac12 m v^2\).

Practice

1. A ball of \(0.50\ \text{kg}\) moves at \(8.0\ \text{m/s}\). (a) Kinetic energy? (b) Thrown at \(16\ \text{m/s}\): kinetic energy, and compared with (a)? (c) Cyclist and bike, \(75\ \text{kg}\), at \(36\ \text{km/h}\): kinetic energy?

ConsiglioSolution
  1. \(\tfrac12 \times 0.50 \times 8.0^2 = 16\ \text{J}\). (b) \(\tfrac12 \times 0.50 \times 16^2 = 64\ \text{J}\): 4 × more, since speed doubled. (c) \(36 \div 3.6 = 10\ \text{m/s}\), so \(E_k = \tfrac12 \times 75 \times 10^2 = 3750\ \text{J}\).

2. (a) A climber of \(60\ \text{kg}\) gains \(200\ \text{m}\) of height: gain in potential energy? (b) A stone of \(0.10\ \text{kg}\) is thrown up at \(12\ \text{m/s}\): how high above the hand? No air.

ConsiglioSolution
  1. \(60 \times 10 \times 200 = 120\,000\ \text{J} = 120\ \text{kJ}\). (b) \(E_k = \tfrac12 \times 0.10 \times 12^2 = 7.2\ \text{J}\). At the top it is all \(E_p\): \(7.2 = 0.10 \times 10 \times \Delta h\), so \(\Delta h = 7.2\ \text{m}\).

3. (a) A crate of \(40\ \text{kg}\) is pushed \(5.0\ \text{m}\) with \(120\ \text{N}\): work done? (b) You carry a bag of \(20\ \text{kg}\) for \(10\ \text{m}\) at constant speed, level: work done by your upward force? (c) Cyclist and bike, \(80\ \text{kg}\), at \(6.0\ \text{m/s}\), brake with \(120\ \text{N}\): stopping distance? And at \(12\ \text{m/s}\)?

ConsiglioSolution
  1. \(W = F d = 600\ \text{J}\). Mass not needed. (b) \(0\ \text{J}\): the force is up, the motion is sideways. (c) \(E_k = \tfrac12 \times 80 \times 6.0^2 = 1440\ \text{J}\), so \(d = 1440 / 120 = 12\ \text{m}\). At \(12\ \text{m/s}\): \(E_k = 5760\ \text{J}\), \(d = 48\ \text{m}\): 4 × more.

Exam-style sheet

\(g = 10\ \text{N/kg}\). Show every step, give units. Total: 33 marks.

1 · Kinetic energy (4). Cyclist and bike, \(80\ \text{kg}\), at \(36\ \text{km/h}\). (a) Speed in \(\text{m/s}\). (b) Kinetic energy. (c) Speed doubles: \(E_k\) changes by what factor?

ConsiglioSolution
  1. \(10\ \text{m/s}\). (b) \(\tfrac12 \times 80 \times 10^2 = 4000\ \text{J}\). (c) × 4 (\(16\,000\ \text{J}\)). Common slips: \(36\) instead of \(10\) (51 840 J), no square (400 J), no \(\tfrac12\) (8000 J).

2 · Potential energy (3). A book of \(1.5\ \text{kg}\) is lifted from the floor to a shelf \(2.0\ \text{m}\) high. (a) Gain in potential energy. (b) It is then pushed \(3.0\ \text{m}\) along the shelf: change in \(E_p\)? Explain.

ConsiglioSolution
  1. \(1.5 \times 10 \times 2.0 = 30\ \text{J}\). (b) \(0\ \text{J}\): \(\Delta h = 0\). The push ends up as thermal energy (friction).

3 · Speed and energy (4). A car of \(1200\ \text{kg}\) drives \(90\ \text{km}\) at \(72\ \text{km/h}\). (a) Time in minutes. (b) Kinetic energy.

ConsiglioSolution
  1. \(90 / 72 = 1.25\ \text{h} = 75\ \text{min}\). (b) \(72 \div 3.6 = 20\ \text{m/s}\), so \(E_k = \tfrac12 \times 1200 \times 20^2 = 240\,000\ \text{J}\).

4 · Roller coaster (5). A trolley of \(400\ \text{kg}\) starts at rest at A, \(20\ \text{m}\) high. B is \(5\ \text{m}\) high, C is at ground level. No friction. (a) \(E_p\) lost from A to B. (b) \(E_k\) and speed at B. (c) Speed at C.

ConsiglioSolution
  1. \(400 \times 10 \times 15 = 60\,000\ \text{J}\). (b) \(E_k = 60\,000\ \text{J}\), so \(v = \sqrt{2 \times 60\,000 / 400} = \sqrt{300} = 17.3\ \text{m/s}\). (c) \(E_k = 400 \times 10 \times 20 = 80\,000\ \text{J}\), so \(v = \sqrt{400} = 20\ \text{m/s}\). Energies add up from A, speeds do not.

5 · Work (4). A student pushes a box of \(25\ \text{kg}\) along a level floor with a constant horizontal force of \(60\ \text{N}\). It moves \(8.0\ \text{m}\) at constant speed. (a) Work done by the student. (b) Friction force, and why. (c) The box gains no kinetic energy: where does the energy go?

ConsiglioSolution
  1. \(60 \times 8.0 = 480\ \text{J}\). Mass not needed. (b) \(60\ \text{N}\): constant speed, so the forces balance. (c) Into the thermal store of box and floor (friction), then it spreads out.

6 · The ramp (4). A crate of \(50\ \text{kg}\) is pushed up a \(4.0\ \text{m}\) ramp with \(150\ \text{N}\) parallel to the ramp. It rises \(1.0\ \text{m}\). (a) Work done. (b) Gain in potential energy. (c) How much work is not stored as \(E_p\), and where does it go?

ConsiglioSolution
  1. \(150 \times 4.0 = 600\ \text{J}\) (distance along the ramp). (b) \(50 \times 10 \times 1.0 = 500\ \text{J}\) (height counts). (c) \(100\ \text{J}\), to the thermal store by friction.

7 · Stopping a truck (5). A truck of \(8000\ \text{kg}\) at \(54\ \text{km/h}\) brakes with a constant \(30\,000\ \text{N}\). (a) Kinetic energy. (b) Stopping distance. (c) At twice the speed: distance? Explain, no full calculation.

ConsiglioSolution
  1. \(54 \div 3.6 = 15\ \text{m/s}\), so \(E_k = \tfrac12 \times 8000 \times 15^2 = 900\,000\ \text{J}\). (b) \(d = 900\,000 / 30\,000 = 30\ \text{m}\). (c) \(120\ \text{m}\): \(E_k \propto v^2\) and \(d \propto E_k\), so × 4, not × 2.

8 · A falling ball (4). A ball of \(0.50\ \text{kg}\) is dropped from \(45\ \text{m}\). No air. (a) \(E_p\) lost. (b) \(E_k\) just before landing. (c) Speed in \(\text{m/s}\) and in \(\text{km/h}\).

ConsiglioSolution
  1. \(0.50 \times 10 \times 45 = 225\ \text{J}\). (b) \(225\ \text{J}\). (c) \(v = \sqrt{2 \times 225 / 0.50} = \sqrt{900} = 30\ \text{m/s}\), and \(30 \times 3.6 = 108\ \text{km/h}\).

Spot the mistake

A car of \(1000\ \text{kg}\) at \(50\ \text{km/h}\) has \(E_k \approx 97\ \text{kJ}\). Three slips that still give a number that looks fine:

  • Speed in \(\text{km/h}\): \(1\,250\,000\ \text{J}\), about 13 × too big.
  • No square: \(6950\ \text{J}\).
  • No \(\tfrac12\): \(193\,000\ \text{J}\), double.

Three checks: units (J, m/s), size (compare with a known case, like the 97 kJ), ratio (speed × 2 means energy × 4).
